## Purpose

Work a business from revenue down to net profit, with the margin at each level.

## Scope

One period: revenue, cost of sales, operating expenses, other income, interest and a tax rate.

## Assumptions

- All figures are for the same period.
- The tax rate entered is the effective rate on taxable profit.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `revenue` | Revenue (£) | number | 500000 | - |
| `cost_of_goods_sold` | Cost of goods sold (£) | number | 200000 | - |
| `operating_expenses` | Operating expenses (£) | number | 150000 | - |
| `other_income` | Other income (£) | number | 0 | - |
| `interest_and_other_costs` | Interest and other costs (£) | number | 0 | - |
| `tax_rate` | Tax rate (%) | number | 19 | Your company's effective rate. Use the Corporation Tax calculator to work it out. |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- The tax rate must be between 0% and 100%.
- Tax is charged on a profit and never on a loss, so a loss-making period shows no tax rather than a refund.

## Formula and logic

Gross profit is revenue less cost of sales. Operating profit deducts operating expenses. Profit before tax adds other income and deducts interest. Tax applies only where profit before tax is positive.

## Outputs

- `gross_profit`
- `gross_margin`
- `operating_profit`
- `operating_margin`
- `profit_before_tax`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Rules and source dependencies

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

A management estimate on the figures entered. It is not a statutory account, a tax computation, or accounting advice, and it follows no particular accounting standard.

## Methodology

Benchmarks were derived independently and include a loss-making period, which proves the no-tax-on-a-loss rule. BUS-003, BUS-004 and BUS-005 share ONE profit computation and differ only in how much of the profit and loss account they expose. Every benchmark scenario is asserted through more than one of them, so the three can never disagree about the same figures.
