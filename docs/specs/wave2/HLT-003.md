## Purpose

Estimate total daily energy expenditure from basal rate and activity.

## Scope

As HLT-002, plus an activity level.

## Assumptions

- Activity multipliers are population averages from the Harris-Benedict tradition.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sex` | Sex | select | "female" | - |
| `age` | Age | number | 30 | - |
| `height` | Height (cm) | number | 165 | - |
| `weight` | Weight (kg) | number | 65 | - |
| `activity` | Activity level | select | "moderate" | Most people overestimate this. Be honest rather than aspirational. |
| `body_fat_percentage` | Body fat (%) if known | number | "" | - |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- Heights outside 50 to 260 cm, weights outside 20 to 400 kg and ages outside 18 to 120 are refused with a message that explains the expected unit, because a height entered in metres or a weight in stones would otherwise produce confident nonsense.

## Formula and logic

Basal rate multiplied by an activity factor from 1.2 for sedentary to 1.9 for extra active.

## Outputs

- `bmr`
- `tdee`
- `activity_factor`
- `calories_from_activity`
- `sedentary_tdee`
- `very_active_tdee`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Rules and source dependencies

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

The activity factor is the LARGEST source of error here, and the calculator shows the sedentary and very active figures side by side so the reader can see that the gap between them dwarfs the difference between the BMR formulas. Most people overestimate their activity level. An estimate from population formulas, not a measurement of an individual, and not medical advice. The calculator points to a GP or a registered dietitian, and specifically says to speak to a GP first where there is any history of an eating disorder.

## Methodology

Each case asserts the sedentary and very active figures as well as the chosen one.
