## Purpose

Estimate the calories used by an activity from its MET value.

## Scope

One activity of a stated MET value, duration and body weight.

## Assumptions

- One MET is one calorie per kilogram per hour.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `met_value` | MET value of the activity | number | 4.3 | Brisk walking is about 4.3, running at 10 km/h about 9.8, gentle cycling about 6. |
| `weight` | Weight (kg) | number | 70 | - |
| `duration_minutes` | Duration (minutes) | number | 45 | - |
| `times_per_week` | Times per week | number | 5 | - |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- A MET value above 25 is refused as beyond elite competition.
- A session longer than a day is refused.

## Formula and logic

MET times weight in kilograms divided by 60 gives calories per minute.

## Outputs

- `calories_burned`
- `calories_per_minute`
- `weekly_if_repeated`
- `equivalent_kg_of_fat`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Rules and source dependencies

The conversion to kilograms of fat uses the ruleset's 7,700 calories per kilogram.

## Product boundary

MET values are population averages for an activity at a stated intensity, and real expenditure varies with fitness, technique and terrain. Fitness trackers routinely overestimate. The figure INCLUDES the energy the body would have used anyway during that time, so it is not all additional. An estimate from population formulas, not a measurement of an individual, and not medical advice. The calculator points to a GP or a registered dietitian, and specifically says to speak to a GP first where there is any history of an eating disorder.
