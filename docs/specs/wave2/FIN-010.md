## Purpose

Show how long a credit card takes to clear at a fixed monthly payment, and what payment clears it within a target time.

## Scope

A single card with a fixed payment and constant APR.

## Assumptions

- No further spending on the card.
- A constant APR.
- Interest charged monthly on the outstanding balance.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `balance` | Card balance (£) | number | 3000 | - |
| `apr` | APR (%) | number | 21.9 | Enter 21.9 for 21.9%. |
| `monthly_payment` | Monthly payment (£) | number | 150 | - |
| `target_months` | Clear it within (months) | number | "" | Optional. We work out the payment needed to hit this target. |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- A payment that never clears the balance is reported as a validation error rather than an infinite term.

## Formula and logic

Payoff by month-by-month simulation. The payment needed for a target term uses the annuity formula `(B x r) / (1 - (1+r)^-n)`.

## Outputs

- `months`
- `total_interest`
- `total_repaid`
- `payment_for_target`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Rules and source dependencies

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

An estimate. Real card interest depends on the issuer's daily calculation, statement dates, promotional rates and how payments are allocated between balance types.

## Methodology

Payoff months cross-checked against the closed form `n = -ln(1 - Br/P)/ln(1+r)`; the oracle reports any disagreement with its own simulation.
