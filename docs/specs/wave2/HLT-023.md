## Purpose

Project upcoming periods from the last one.

## Scope

Last period, cycle length, period length and a number of cycles.

## Assumptions

- Every cycle is the same length, which almost nobody's is.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `last_period_date` | First day of your last period | text | "2026-06-01" | In the form YYYY-MM-DD. |
| `cycle_length` | Average cycle length (days) | number | 28 | - |
| `period_length` | Period length (days) | number | 5 | - |
| `cycles_to_show` | Cycles to show | number | 6 | - |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- Cycle lengths outside 20 to 45 days and period lengths outside 1 to 14 days are refused, the latter noting that longer bleeding is worth discussing with a GP.

## Formula and logic

Each period starts one cycle length after the previous one.

## Outputs

- `next_period_date`
- `next_period_ends`
- `cycle_length_used`
- `period_length_used`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

- **Short period** - {"last_period_date":"2026-06-05","cycle_length":30,"period_length":3,"cycles_to_show":4}
- **Cycle at the upper end of usual** - {"last_period_date":"2026-05-20","cycle_length":35,"period_length":5,"cycles_to_show":2}

## Rules and source dependencies

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

The assumption of a constant cycle is the weakness, so the dates are a rough guide. Cycles shorter than 24 days or longer than 35 are flagged as outside the usual range. Unusually heavy or painful bleeding, marked changes, or bleeding between periods are all worth discussing with a GP. Not a test and not a diagnosis.

## Methodology

Independently derived from epoch day arithmetic. Date-relative outputs are covered by unit tests rather than benchmarks.
