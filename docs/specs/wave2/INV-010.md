## Purpose

Report arithmetic and geometric average returns from a series of annual returns.

## Scope

A list of yearly percentage returns.

## Assumptions

- Returns are annual and in order.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `returns` | Annual returns (%) | text | "[20 | A list of yearly percentage returns, e.g. [8,-3,12]. |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- A return of -100% or lower is rejected because it destroys the compounding chain.

## Formula and logic

Arithmetic is the simple mean. Geometric is (product of (1+r))^(1/n) - 1.

## Outputs

- `arithmetic_mean`
- `geometric_mean`
- `cumulative_return`
- `difference`
- `years`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Rules and source dependencies

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

The geometric mean is what an investor actually earned; the arithmetic mean always overstates it when returns vary. Both are shown so neither can mislead alone.

## Methodology

Independently derived in the oracle by explicit simulation or summation rather than the closed forms the engine uses.
