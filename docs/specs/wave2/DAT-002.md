## Purpose

Add or subtract a period of years, months and days from a date.

## Scope

One start date and any combination of years, months and days, in either direction.

## Assumptions

- The Gregorian calendar throughout, with no allowance for the 1752 changeover.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `start_date` | Start date | text | "2026-01-31" | In the form YYYY-MM-DD. |
| `operation` | Add or subtract? | select | "add" | - |
| `years` | Years | number | 0 | - |
| `months` | Months | number | 1 | - |
| `days` | Days | number | 0 | - |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- A malformed or impossible date is refused with the expected ISO format.
- The three period fields may be mixed freely and may be zero.

## Formula and logic

Years and months are applied first, then days. Where the resulting day of the month does not exist, the date is clamped to the last day of that month.

## Outputs

- `result_date`
- `day_of_week`
- `clamped_to_month_end`
- `is_leap_year`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Edge cases covered by benchmarks

- **Add days across a month end** - {"start_date":"2026-01-28","years":0,"months":0,"days":5,"operation":"add"}
- **Add one month to 31 January, which clamps to the month end** - {"start_date":"2026-01-31","years":0,"months":1,"days":0,"operation":"add"}
- **Add one year to a leap day** - {"start_date":"2028-02-29","years":1,"months":0,"days":0,"operation":"add"}
- **Subtract months across a year boundary** - {"start_date":"2026-03-15","years":0,"months":5,"days":0,"operation":"subtract"}
- **Add years, months and days together** - {"start_date":"2026-06-10","years":2,"months":3,"days":20,"operation":"add"}
- **Subtract days across a year boundary** - {"start_date":"2026-01-05","years":0,"months":0,"days":10,"operation":"subtract"}
- **Add one month to 31 May, which clamps to 30 June** - {"start_date":"2026-05-31","years":0,"months":1,"days":0,"operation":"add"}

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

MONTH ARITHMETIC IS NOT ASSOCIATIVE AND THE ORDER MATTERS. Adding one month to 31 January gives 28 February in a common year, not 3 March, and the clamping is reported through a dedicated output and a warning rather than left for the reader to notice. Applying the days before the months would give a different answer for the same inputs, so the order is fixed, documented and asserted in tests. All calendar arithmetic is performed in UTC rather than in the browser's local time zone. A date calculator that used local time would give a different answer to a reader in Sydney than to one in London for the same inputs, and would shift by an hour twice a year in Britain alone.

## Methodology

The oracle builds result dates by counted day numbers from a fixed epoch and by explicit month-length tables, never by the Date object the engine uses, so a shared library bug could not hide. Month-end clamping is asserted in both directions and across a leap year.
