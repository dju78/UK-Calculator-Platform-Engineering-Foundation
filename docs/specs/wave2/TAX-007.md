## Purpose

Convert an annual salary into the equivalent hourly, daily, weekly and monthly gross pay.

## Scope

Gross pay conversion on a stated working pattern. No tax or National Insurance is applied.

## Assumptions

- Pay is spread evenly across the hours entered.
- Defaults of 37.5 hours and 52 paid weeks apply only when the user supplies neither.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `annual_salary` | Annual salary (£) | number | 30000 | - |
| `hours_per_week` | Hours per week | number | 37.5 | - |
| `paid_weeks_per_year` | Paid weeks per year | number | 52 | - |
| `days_per_week` | Days per week | number | 5 | - |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- Hours must be above zero and paid weeks between 1 and 53.

## Formula and logic

Hourly pay is `annual salary / (hours per week x paid weeks per year)`. This is the exact inverse of TAX-006 on the same pattern, and shares the same conversion layer rather than reimplementing it.

## Outputs

- `annual_salary`
- `monthly_salary`
- `weekly_pay`
- `daily_pay`
- `hourly_rate`
- `annual_hours`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Rules and source dependencies

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

Gross pay before any deductions. Salaried work often involves unpaid additional hours, which would lower the true hourly rate.

## Methodology

Every benchmark is the inverse of the matching TAX-006 case, so a rounding or convention drift between the two directions would fail immediately.
