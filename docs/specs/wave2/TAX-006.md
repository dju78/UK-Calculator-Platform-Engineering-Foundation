## Purpose

Convert an hourly rate into the equivalent annual, monthly, weekly and daily gross pay.

## Scope

Gross pay conversion on a stated working pattern. No tax or National Insurance is applied.

## Assumptions

- Every paid week is worked at the stated hours.
- Paid weeks per year default to 52 and hours per week to 37.5 only when the user has supplied neither.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hourly_rate` | Hourly rate (£) | number | 15 | - |
| `hours_per_week` | Hours per week | number | 37.5 | - |
| `paid_weeks_per_year` | Paid weeks per year | number | 52 | Use 52 for a normal contract, or fewer for term-time-only work. |
| `days_per_week` | Days per week | number | 5 | - |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- Hours per week are required, because an hourly rate cannot be annualised without them; the calculator refuses rather than assuming a figure the user never saw.
- Hours must be above zero and paid weeks between 1 and 53.

## Formula and logic

Annual pay is `rate x hours per week x paid weeks per year`. Every other frequency is derived from that single annual figure, so the four views can never disagree with one another.

## Outputs

- `annual_salary`
- `monthly_salary`
- `weekly_pay`
- `daily_pay`
- `hourly_rate`
- `annual_hours`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in
`apps/web/src/components/calculators/outputFormats.ts`, per calculator and per
key, so ratios render as percentages and money as `£1,234.56`.

## Rules and source dependencies

Not rules-sensitive. No statutory values are used.

## Product boundary

Gross pay before any deductions. Unpaid leave, sickness and variable rotas will change the annual figure.

## Methodology

Benchmarks were derived by direct arithmetic on the stated pattern, independent of the engine, and each case is asserted twice: once forwards through TAX-006 and once backwards through TAX-007, so the pair must be exact inverses.
