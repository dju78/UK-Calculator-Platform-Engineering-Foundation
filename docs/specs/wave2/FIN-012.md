## Purpose

Compare keeping several debts against consolidating them into one loan.

## Scope

Existing revolving or fixed-payment debts against a single fixed-rate consolidation loan.

## Assumptions

- Existing debts are repaid at their current monthly payments with no further borrowing.
- The consolidation loan is fixed rate and fully amortising.

## Inputs

| Name | Label | Type | Default | Units / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `debts` | Existing debts | text | '[{"balance":3000 | One entry per debt: balance, APR as a percentage, and monthly payment. |
| `consolidation_apr` | Consolidation loan APR (%) | number | 9.9 | - |
| `consolidation_years` | Consolidation term (years) | number | 5 | - |
| `fee` | Arrangement fee (£) | number | 0 | - |

Percentages are entered as human percentages (5 means 5%) and normalised once
inside the engine.

## Validation

Enforced centrally at the engine boundary and by the shared validation module:

- Any non-finite input is rejected with a readable message.
- Any non-finite output is rejected rather than returned, so `NaN` and
  `Infinity` can never reach a user.
- Terms are bounded (maximum 150 years) and monetary amounts capped, so no
  input can spin an amortisation loop indefinitely.
- An existing debt whose payment never clears its balance is reported explicitly, naming the debt.

## Formula and logic

Each existing debt is run to payoff by simulation to obtain its total interest. The consolidation loan uses the annuity formula on the combined balance plus any fee.

## Outputs

- `current_payoff_months`
- `consolidated_monthly_payment`

Formatting is decided by the central registry in

## Product boundary

Not a credit decision. The comparison deliberately reports the total interest change as well as the monthly payment change, because a longer consolidation term routinely lowers the monthly payment while increasing the total cost.
